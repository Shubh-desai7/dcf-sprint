Case / 1. About Wanderly

Duffels for the weekend, sold two ways

Wanderly is an Indian travel essentials brand founded in April 2023. It sells premium duffels and travel organisers through twenty-six of its own stores and its own website and app. FY26 closed at ₹145.0 crore of revenue and ₹12.3 crore of EBITDA.

The business

Wanderly makes premium canvas and leather duffels, weekenders and garment bags priced between ₹2,999 and ₹11,999, alongside a fast-growing organiser line of packing cubes, toiletry kits, tech pouches and passport wallets. Bags are about 72% of revenue and accessories the rest, and accessories are growing faster.

Two verticals

Offline retail stores. Twenty-six stores that the company owns and runs, in airport terminals and premium malls. Walk-in customers cost nothing to acquire, so performance marketing is light. Against that, every store pays rent and staff whether it sells or not.

D2C website and app. It carries no rent, but it pays for every order it wins through performance marketing, and it pays to ship, pack and collect the money.

There is no third vertical. Wanderly does not sell on marketplaces, does not distribute through multi-brand outlets and does not franchise.

Case / 2. The mandate

What the investor wants

From
Rohan Mehta, Managing Partner, Northstar Ventures
To
Investment team
Subject
Wanderly - how I want this one valued

Team,

Wanderly is a three-year-old Indian brand: premium travel duffels and organisers. It sells through two verticals it owns outright - twenty-six retail stores and its D2C website and app. No marketplaces, no distributors, no franchise. FY26 closed at ₹145.0 crore of revenue, ₹75.0 crore offline and ₹70.0 crore D2C, with ₹12.3 crore of EBITDA. Founder Aditya Sen is raising equity and wants a valuation of ₹115 crore.

Discount rate. Our required return is 22% a year. Run the sensitivity at 20%, 22% and 24%, against terminal growth of 3%, 4% and 5%. Four percent is the base.

Forecast FY27 to FY30 and value the company as at 31 March 2026. Four years, no more.

Model the two verticals separately, and split each into bags and accessories. They behave differently. Assumptions for each are in the pack.

The margin stack, in both verticals. Revenue less cost of goods sold gives gross margin. Take off packaging and payment gateway charges - and shipping in D2C, which the stores do not carry - and that gives CM1. Take off performance marketing and that gives CM2. In the stores, rent and staff sit below CM2, and taking them off gives the store contribution.

Rent and staff are fixed costs. Do not run them as a percentage of revenue - grow the FY26 rupee amount year on year. They grow slower than revenue, which is what makes the store margin expand.

Common costs, line by line. Salaries, brand marketing, technology and administration each have their own growth rate in the pack. No single blanket rate for the head office.

Working capital moves with revenue, one for one. Hold it at 20% of revenue and treat the increase as cash out. Working capital at 31 March 2026 was ₹29 crore.

One lump of capex. A warehouse racking and automated packing line, ₹8 crore in total, all in FY27, depreciated over four years. Depreciation on the existing fit-outs continues at ₹3.5 crore a year.

Debt. The company carries a ₹25 crore term loan at 11% a year. Hold it constant across the forecast. EBITDA less depreciation is EBIT; less interest is profit before tax. Tax at 25%, and no tax in a loss year. Cash on the balance sheet is ₹12 crore.

Value the equity with free cash flow to equity. Then give me the enterprise value, the NPV against the founder's ask of ₹115 crore, and the IRR we earn if we pay the ask.

Three cases. Your cash flow model is the base. The upside is 1.2 times it and the downside is 0.8 times it.

Come back with the equity valuation of the company using the discounted cash flow.

Rohan Mehta
Managing Partner, Northstar Ventures
