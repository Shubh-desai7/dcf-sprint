Case / 1. About Cool Nomad

Bags built for the road, sold two ways

Cool Nomad is an Indian travel goods brand founded in April 2023. It sells hard-shell luggage and travel accessories through twenty-two of its own stores and its own website and app. FY26 closed at ₹150 crore of revenue and ₹17.6 crore of EBITDA.

The business

Cool Nomad makes polycarbonate hard-shell cabin and check-in luggage priced between ₹3,499 and ₹9,999, alongside a growing accessories line of laptop sleeves, weekend duffels, packing cubes and travel wallets. Bags are three quarters of revenue and accessories a quarter, and accessories are growing faster.

Two verticals

Offline retail stores. Twenty-two stores that the company owns and runs, in airport terminals and metro malls. Walk-in customers cost nothing to acquire and the basket is larger, so gross margin is the best in the business. Against that, every store pays rent and staff whether it sells or not.

D2C website and app. The larger vertical. It carries no rent, but it pays for every order it wins through performance marketing, and it pays to ship, pack and collect the money.

There is no third vertical. Cool Nomad does not sell on marketplaces, does not distribute through multi-brand outlets and does not franchise.

Case / 2. The mandate

What the investor wants

From
Arjun Varma, Managing Partner, Meridian Growth Partners
To
Investment team
Subject
Cool Nomad - how I want this one valued

Team,

Cool Nomad is a three-year-old Indian luggage brand. Hard-shell cabin and check-in bags, plus a growing accessories line. It sells through two verticals it owns outright - twenty-two retail stores and its D2C website and app. No marketplaces, no distributors, no franchise. FY26 closed at ₹150 crore of revenue, ₹70 crore offline and ₹80 crore D2C, with ₹17.6 crore of EBITDA. Founder Kabir Rao is raising equity to open stores and fund inventory.

Discount rate. Our required return is 20% a year. Run the sensitivity at 18%, 20% and 22%, against terminal growth of 4%, 5% and 6%. Five percent is the base.

Forecast FY27 to FY30 and value the company as at 31 March 2026. Four years, no more.

Model the two verticals separately, and split each into bags and accessories. They behave differently, and you will need the bags and accessories revenue lines at the end for the comparables. Assumptions for each are in the pack.

The margin stack, in both verticals. Revenue less cost of goods sold gives gross margin, near 50%. Take off packaging and payment gateway charges - and shipping in D2C, which the stores do not carry - and that gives CM1, near 48%. Take off performance marketing and that gives CM2. In the stores, rent and staff sit below CM2, and taking them off gives the store contribution. Each vertical contributes about 20% of its own revenue in FY26.

Rent and staff are fixed costs. A lease escalates whether the store has a good quarter or a bad one, and a store is manned to a roster, not to footfall. Do not run them as a percentage of revenue - grow the FY26 rupee amount year on year, for escalation and salary increases plus the new stores. They grow slower than revenue, which is what makes the store margin expand.

Common costs, line by line. Salaries, brand marketing, technology and administration do not behave alike, so each has its own growth rate in the pack. No single blanket rate for the head office.

Working capital moves with revenue, one for one. If revenue rises 20%, working capital rises 20%. Hold it at 18% of revenue and treat the increase as cash out. FY26 earned ₹10.9 crore of profit after tax and threw off ₹0.4 crore of cash - that is the business model, not an accounting quirk.

One lump of capex. Twelve baggage engraving machines at ₹0.5 crore each, ₹6 crore, all in FY28, depreciated over five years. Depreciation on the existing fit-outs continues at ₹3 crore a year. The company has no debt, so EBITDA less depreciation is profit before tax, tax at 25%, and that is profit after tax.

Then value it a second way. Once the cash flow model is done and written down, value the company off the listed comparables, on revenue and on profit. They are in the locked section - do not open it until your model is finished.

Do the comparables as a sum of the parts. Apply your chosen multiple to the bags revenue and 0.8 times that multiple to accessories. Accessories are a more crowded category with weaker pricing power, and a rupee of accessories revenue is worth less to me than a rupee of bags revenue.

Three cases. Your cash flow model is the base. The upside is 1.2 times it and the downside is 0.8 times it.

Come back with the equity valuation of the company using the discounted cash flow.

Arjun Varma
Managing Partner, Meridian Growth Partners
